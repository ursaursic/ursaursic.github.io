About Me

Hey there! I'm Urša, a postdoctoral scientist in the field of experimental biophysics. I love working with cells and subcellular structures to understand biological processes through the lens of physics. Throughout my life I have been excited about solving riddles and exploring the unknown. This curiosity drives my scientific endeavors, however, I strive to discover new insights that lead to meaningful contributions to the world.

A mechanical ratchet drives unilateral cytokinesis

10.1038/s41586-023-06494-6

This is a cool Nature paper together with the lead author Alison Kickuth. It describes the mechanical mechanisms underlying unilateral cell division. My part of the project was to measure and analyze the material properties of the cytoplasm. We have shown that the changes in material properties of the cytoplasm allow the actin band to form properly during interphase and is able to ingress in M-phase.

Role: Lead researcher

From coffee stains to uniform deposits: Significance of the contact-line mobility

10.1038/s41586-023-06495-5

This project explores the role of contact-line mobility in the formation of uniform deposits. We investigate the physical principles governing the spreading and drying of liquid droplets on solid surfaces.

FameLab

FameLab is an international science communication competition where researchers have three minutes to explain a scientific topic to a general audience, with no slides allowed, just physical props one can carry onto the stage.

Science Goes to School

Science Goes to School is an initiative that brings researchers into nearby schools to meet and engage with students. Besides sharing the excitement of science and showing what a career in research really looks like, it is also about breaking down prejudice: most of the visiting researchers come from all over the world, so the students get to know international scientists in person. In this way, the initiative helps promote science while standing up against xenophobia and racism.
